## The default layout

When tt-git starts, the screen is split in two:

- The **graph** takes the top 70%.
- **Status** and **branches** sit side by side below it.
- The **help bar** is the last line.

The **details** pane is hidden until you ask for it. When you press
<kbd>space</kbd> on a commit, details takes the place of the bottom row, and
<kbd>space</kbd> again puts status and branches back.

Each pane's title starts with its number. Press <kbd>1</kbd>, <kbd>2</kbd>,
<kbd>3</kbd> or <kbd>4</kbd> to focus it, or <kbd>tab</kbd> to move to the
next one. The focused pane has a green border and a bold title.

<Aside type="tip">
	The layout is yours to change. You can move the panes, resize them, or keep
	details on screen all the time. See [Layout](/configuration/layout/).
</Aside>

## Graph

{/* screenshot: zoom on the graph pane (top region), callouts on: the title "[1] Graph [all] 1/120" (scope and position), a lane line, a merge ◉, the green current-branch badge, a [local] badge, an [origin/…] badge, a <tag>, and the author / date / hash columns on the right. */}

The graph shows the history of every branch, remote branch and tag, newest
commit first. Each line of development keeps its own column and color, merges
are drawn as `◉` and other commits as `●`. Badges on a row name the branches
and tags that point at it: the checked-out branch on a green background,
local branches in `[brackets]`, remote branches like `[origin/main]`, and tags
like `<v1.0>`.

The title shows what's loaded and where you are: `Graph [all] 1/120` means
all branches, first of 120 commits. Press <kbd>a</kbd> to show only the
current branch and its upstream.

Read more in [Graph](/panes/graph/).

## Status

{/* screenshot: zoom on the status pane, callouts on the title "Status: repo · main → origin/main ↑1" (branch, upstream, ahead/behind) and on the two-letter codes: "M " staged (green), " M" modified (red), "??" untracked. */}

The status pane lists every changed file with the same two-letter code as
`git status -s`. The title shows the repository, the current branch, its
upstream and how far ahead or behind it is.

Press <kbd>space</kbd> to stage or unstage a file, <kbd>c</kbd> to commit,
and <kbd>enter</kbd> to open a file and stage it a hunk or line at a time.
Read more in [Status](/panes/status/).

## Branches

{/* screenshot: zoom on the branches pane, callouts on the tab row in the title (Branches · Tags · Unmerged · Merged), the "* " marker on the current branch, and the status column values ✓, ↑2, local, origin, gone. */}

One list holds your local and remote branches, with `main` and `origin/main`
on a single row. The branches you've used most recently are at the top. The
column after the name says how each one relates to its remote: `✓` in sync,
`↑2` two commits to push, `local` never pushed, `gone` deleted upstream.

Press <kbd>enter</kbd> to check a branch out. <kbd>]</kbd> and <kbd>[</kbd>
switch to the Tags, Unmerged, Merged and Stash tabs. Read more in
[Branches](/panes/branches/).

## Details

{/* screenshot: full screen with details open in place of the bottom row after pressing space on a commit; callouts on the commit message, the author/hash line and a diff hunk. */}

Details shows whatever is selected: a commit's message and diff, a stash, or
the diff of a file in the status pane. It follows your selection as you move.

Press <kbd>space</kbd> in the graph to show or hide it, or <kbd>enter</kbd>
to open the commit fullscreen with its changed files beside the diff. Read
more in [Details](/panes/details/).

## The help bar

{/* screenshot: zoom on the bottom line, callouts on the pane's key hints (left), the global hints (tab next pane, ? help, q quit), and a "✓ commit" notice on the right. */}

The last line lists the most useful keys for the focused pane, followed by
<kbd>tab</kbd>, <kbd>?</kbd> and <kbd>q</kbd>, which work everywhere. It
changes as you move between panes. Keys that don't fit are left off; press
<kbd>?</kbd> for the full list.

On the right, the help bar shows what tt-git is doing: `push…` while a push
runs, then `✓ push` when it's done. When something fails, an **Error** box
opens with git's own message. Press <kbd>enter</kbd> or <kbd>esc</kbd> to
close it.
